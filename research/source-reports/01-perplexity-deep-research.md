## Executive assessment

You were right to challenge the earlier framing. If the question is **“how much end-customers spend on bathrooms and wardrobes?”**, the numbers can get very large very quickly. But if the question is **“how much revenue is generated by the companies that actually site-measure, supply and install shower screens, wardrobe doors, mirrors and splashbacks?”**, the market is materially smaller. The spend and the company revenue line up at the job level, but they are **not** the same once you filter out builders, wholesale-only suppliers, DIY sales, and broader glazing/joinery categories. Company websites and LinkedIn profiles show that many relevant businesses are specialist installers, while others such as Pivotech and Danmac are channel suppliers or wholesale-focused and should not sit inside your strict target market. citeturn43search5turn43search9turn43search13turn44search16turn47search11turn31search4turn31search1

## What should count and what should not

For your purposes, the cleanest market definition is:

**Primary market**
Companies that directly **site-measure, quote, custom-manufacture or source, and install** shower screens, wardrobe doors, built-in wardrobes, flush-face mirrors, mirrors and splashbacks. Regency, Premium, Betta, Monaro, Civic, Cesana and similar firms belong here. citeturn43search9turn43search5turn46search6turn43search10turn13search0turn43search13

**Excluded from the strict primary market**
Wholesale-only or installer-channel suppliers, even if they are important ecosystem players. Pivotech explicitly describes itself as supplying wholesale shower screens and wardrobe systems to installers, and Danmac’s LinkedIn listing is also wholesale/kit-form oriented. Those businesses matter as routes to market, but not as your end-channel customer if your ICP is the installer itself. citeturn44search16turn47search11

**Secondary market**
Wardrobe carcasses, built-in storage, cabinet carcasses and adjacent joinery manufacturers/installers. This overlaps with wardrobes, but it is not a perfect one-for-one match with the primary shower-screen channel. Freedom Kitchens & Wardrobes, Claytons, Imperial Kitchens, Almara Cabinets and similar firms sit here. citeturn40search0turn41search1turn40search1turn41search0

That distinction matters because the broad Australian **Glazing Services** industry is **A$3.8 billion**, and **Aluminium Door, Window & Other Fixture Manufacturing** is **A$5.3 billion**. Those are real company-revenue pools, but they are much broader than your niche. If someone gets to a gigantic TAM by starting there and only loosely filtering, the number will be inflated. citeturn31search4turn32search3turn32search1

## TAM, SAM, SOM and Victorian exclusivity

I am treating your “SAM10” wording as **TAM / SAM / SOM**.

For a Victorian launch, I would model it like this:

**Australian primary TAM**  
**A$220m-A$360m** annual revenue pool for the installer companies you actually want to sell into. This is the national pool of relevant companies, not homeowner spend. citeturn34search0turn35search0turn17view1turn43search1turn43search10turn36search5turn20view2turn43search7turn46search6turn47search18turn47search17turn13search0turn43search13turn47search7

**Victorian SAM**  
Because you are launching in Victoria and many of the strongest public comparables are either Victorian or have Victorian presence — Premium, Stegbar, Crystal Home Concepts, Pipers, Executive and national brands servicing Victoria — the **initial serviceable market in Victoria** looks like **roughly A$45m-A$80m** of annual channel revenue on a strict definition. That is an inference from the Victorian-addressable subset of the primary list, not a government statistic, so I would use it as a commercial working range rather than a board-grade audited number. citeturn11search0turn47search15turn13search13turn47search18turn47search6turn34search10

**Early SOM**  
Without your actual product price or attach rate, I cannot honestly convert that to your own revenue. What I can say is that if a new supplier wins only a small share of the Victorian installer channel, the numbers move fast:
- **2% of Victorian SAM** = about **A$0.9m-A$1.6m** of channel revenue influenced
- **5% of Victorian SAM** = about **A$2.3m-A$4.0m**
- **10% of Victorian SAM** = about **A$4.5m-A$8.0m**

That is **channel revenue**, not your own revenue. To convert to your revenue, multiply by your product’s share of each job or by average annual spend per installer account.

**Opportunity cost of granting Victorian exclusivity to someone else**  
This is the commercial hit if you are locked out of Victoria:
- **Lost channel access:** about **A$45m-A$80m** annually in your launch-state serviceable market.
- **Lost supplier revenue ceiling:**  
  - at **2% take-rate**, about **A$0.9m-A$1.6m** a year  
  - at **5% take-rate**, about **A$2.3m-A$4.0m** a year  
  - at **10% take-rate**, about **A$4.5m-A$8.0m** a year

The blunt read: if Victoria is your launch state, **granting exclusivity there is not a minor concession**. It likely hands away the most operationally reachable piece of your Australian market before you have even built density. That is not fatal if the exclusivity partner is massively strategic and guarantees adoption. But if they do not, you are voluntarily shrinking the market you can actually execute against first. citeturn11search0turn47search15turn13search13turn47search18turn47search6turn34search10

## Open questions and limitations

A few things are incomplete, and I do not want to blur them.

Private-company revenue disclosure in this sector is poor. For many firms, publicly available sources show either an estimated revenue figure or only the company size and footprint, not audited accounts. I have used public company-profile estimates where available and clearly marked inferred bands where not. citeturn17view1turn20view2turn36search0turn36search5turn46search9turn47search17

Texas is the main overseas gap. I found a public growth snippet for Texas glazing contractors, but not a citable top-line revenue number from the free source that surfaced publicly. So I have not forced a number into the report. citeturn30search4

The single biggest modelling judgement is **how much of diversified brands like Stegbar should be counted**. If you count their full revenue, the market inflates. If you count only the product families you care about, the market drops materially. In my view, the second approach is the right one for your sales plan. citeturn34search0turn34search11turn31search1

So the real bottom line is this: **your strict Australian target market is real, but it is a low-hundreds-of-millions company-revenue market, not a billion-plus niche.** Victoria matters enough that giving away exclusivity there should only happen if the commercial upside is overwhelming. Otherwise you are handicapping the most reachable part of your launch before the race starts.
